My approach

Start with the business problem, not the tool

SEO is the discipline. Growth is the objective. I follow the same 7 steps on every account. Pick a step to see what I do and what I produce.

Step 1: Understand the business

What does the business need? Learn the business model, customers, markets and goals before opening any tool.

What I evaluate: Business model, Revenue and conversion goals, Target customers and markets, Acquisition channels, Current performance.

What I produce: Business model, audience, objectives, revenue model and target market, written down as a definition of success.

Why it matters: Every later decision is judged against the business goal, not against rankings alone.

Example (Kesariya Farm): A pan-India B2C milk brand needed both search demand and paid reach, so SEO, Google Ads and Meta Ads were planned together. Read the case study

Step 2: Diagnose and audit

What is limiting performance? Audit SEO, tracking, acquisition and the store itself to find the real cause, not just the symptom.

What I evaluate: Technical SEO audit, Keyword and competitor analysis, GA4 conversion funnel analysis, Google Ads account review, Content gap analysis, Tracking and attribution assessment.

What I produce: A short list of real problems, with the evidence for each, not the longest audit.

Why it matters: Fixing the real cause saves budget that would otherwise go to symptoms.

Example (Rivulet): On five platforms, I checked what GTM, Meta and Google Ads were actually recording before trusting any report. Read the case study

Step 3: Prioritize opportunities

What should be fixed first? Score each issue by impact, effort and confidence, then sort it into Now, Next or Later.

What I evaluate: Impact: how much could it change the outcome, Effort: how long will it take, Confidence: how strong is the evidence.

What I produce: A ranked list based on impact, effort, resources and expected outcome: Now, Next and Later.

Why it matters: Limited time and budget go to the work most likely to change leads or revenue.

Example (Neat Everyday): Tracking came first, then content and funnels, so every later decision was judged on real data. Read the case study

Step 4: Build the strategy

What is the plan? Turn the ranked findings into a roadmap the team can follow, with an owner and a success metric for each item.

What I evaluate: Technical SEO and site structure, Content opportunities by search demand, Tracking improvements, Conversion optimization, Paid acquisition.

What I produce: An SEO roadmap, content strategy, paid media plan and measurement framework.

Why it matters: The team knows what to do, in what order and how success will be measured.

Example (ManekTech): Content and landing pages were mapped to TOFU, MOFU and BOFU stages for lead-generation sites. Read the case study

Step 5: Execute and implement

Who builds it? Work with developers, content writers, designers and paid media specialists, and implement directly where needed.

What I evaluate: Developers, Content teams, Designers, Paid media, Marketing and leadership.

What I produce: Technical fixes, content optimization and campaign changes live on the site, not just recommendations.

Why it matters: Plans become live changes, so results can actually appear.

Example (Atlas Softweb): I coordinated the content writer, designers and developers across SEO and paid campaigns. Read the case study

Step 6: Measure performance

What changed? Compare results against the original goal, using the tracking set up in the earlier steps.

What I evaluate: Organic traffic, Qualified leads, Conversions and revenue, CPA and ROAS, Rankings and impressions.

What I produce: A report tied to the original goal.

Why it matters: Results are tied to leads, revenue and cost, not only traffic.

Example (Neat Everyday): Looker Studio dashboards and Google Cloud API reports pulled from GA4, Search Console and YouTube. Read the case study

Step 7: Optimize and scale

What comes next? Learn why the result happened, then feed it back into the next round of priorities.

What I evaluate: Experiments and A/B tests, Performance reviews, Opportunity identification, Scaling what works.

What I produce: The next round of priorities, ready to start.

Why it matters: Each cycle makes the next one more accurate, so what works can be scaled.

Example (Equest Solutions): A/B tests were checked against the rebuilt funnel data, which led to a 25% conversion lift in the first quarter. Read the case study

AEO, GEO and AI search

Beyond traditional search: optimizing for the AI search ecosystem

People now find answers through Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity as well as search results. I improve how easily a brand can be found, understood and trusted in both. At Neat Everyday I am layering AEO and schema markup on the site.

Google AI Overviews

Summaries shown above search results. Pages need clear, direct answers and trustworthy sources to be used.

ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity

AI tools that answer questions directly. They draw on well-structured, authoritative pages and brand mentions.

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)

Writing pages that answer real questions in a clear format that search and AI tools can lift out.

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)

Making a brand and its content easy for generative AI tools to understand and reference.

Entity and brand authority

Consistent brand, product and author information across the site and the web so the business is clearly identified.

Structured data and schema

Schema markup that labels products, FAQs, articles and organizations in a form machines can read.

Topical authority

Covering a subject in depth with connected pages, so the site is seen as a reliable source.

Content structure and access

Clear headings, short answers, crawlable text and fast pages, so information is easy to reach and read.

Working principles

Six rules I work by, each with where I applied it.

Business results before vanity metrics

Traffic and rankings matter, but revenue, leads and cost per customer decide whether the work succeeded. Kesariya Farm was judged on monthly revenue, which grew from ₹50K to ₹5L.

Diagnose before prescribing

Find the cause of a problem before recommending a fix. At Rivulet I checked what each platform was actually recording before changing anything.

Prioritize over overwhelm

A short, ranked plan beats a 200-item audit nobody finishes. At Neat Everyday, tracking came first, then content and funnels.

Measurement is part of the strategy

If tracking is unreliable, every decision rests on guesses. At Equest I built funnels and GA4 tracking before running any A/B test.

Recommendations need execution

A good plan only helps once someone builds it, so I work with the people who implement it. At Atlas I coordinated the content writer, designers and developers.

AI speeds up the work, people make the decisions

I use AI for research, analysis and drafting. Strategy, priorities and final decisions stay with me.
